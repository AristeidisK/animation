The rule

Every subscription is bought at the gate that needs it, monthly, never annually. You asked to know whether this works before committing real money — a tool stack bought up front quietly converts that question into a sunk cost you will then feel obliged to justify.

The good news: Gate 0 costs nothing. You already own everything it needs. The first real money is roughly $150, and it arrives only after you have heard your own script read aloud in Greek and decided it is worth continuing.

The whole first year, at a sustained four videos a month, lands near $1,300–1,700 — and every part of it is cancellable monthly.

Part five

Do not buy

  • No
    JIRA. You named it, so let me argue properly. JIRA is built for engineering teams coordinating sprints across dependencies. Your unit of work is an episode moving through a fixed eight-beat pipeline — already described in the style bible, already tracked by files on disk. The cost is not the licence, it is the ceremony: boards, workflows and ticket hygiene that consume hours you do not have at a headcount of one. GitHub Issues plus Projects is free, lives beside the code, and will hold until you are three or more people. Your familiarity with Atlassian from work is real, but familiarity with a tool is not the same as it being right at this scale.
  • No
    Suno or any AI music generator. Two independent reasons. The free tier grants no commercial rights at all, and fully AI-generated audio is not eligible for Content ID, so you could never protect a cue you liked. Layered on top is unresolved label litigation. A licensed library costs $15/month and removes the entire category of risk.
  • No
    Adobe Creative Cloud, Canva Pro, or a video editor subscription. Assembly is ffmpeg driven by Claude Code, and everything compositable is a Figma component. Buy an editor only if you find yourself fighting ffmpeg for hours — and note that would itself be a finding worth acting on.
  • Later
    A domain and channel name. The name is deliberately deferred until after Gate 1. Buying a domain now anchors you to a name chosen before you had seen any finished video.
  • Later
    A company, business bank account or accountant. Premature until there is revenue or a second person taking equity. Revisit at Gate 3.
  • Later
    Annual billing on anything. The discount is real and it is a trap here. Monthly billing is what keeps the kill criteria honest.

Part six

Things you did not ask about, and need

1. A dedicated Google account — do this first

Not your work account, and not your main personal one. The channel, the Google Cloud project, the OAuth credentials and the API quota all hang off a single Google identity, and disentangling that later is genuinely painful. Create it before the channel exists. When your wife joins, YouTube Brand Account permissions let you grant her access without ever sharing a password.

Do not skip

A channel created under your CEPR work account is a channel your employer has a plausible claim to look at, and one you could lose access to if you changed jobs. Ten free minutes now.

2. Somewhere for the generated assets to live

Frames, clips and renders are deliberately excluded from git — they are large and reproducible. But "reproducible" means paying fal.ai again, so treat them as worth keeping. A single external SSD covers the pilot phase for a one-off cost; cloud object storage runs a few dollars a month per terabyte if you would rather not think about it. Either is fine. Having neither is how you lose Pilot A.

3. A channel email address

Comes free with the dedicated Google account, and is what goes in the channel's About page for licensing and brand enquiries. Which, per the brief, is where the actual money is more likely to come from than advertising.
